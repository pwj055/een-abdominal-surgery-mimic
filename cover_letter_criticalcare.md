## Why this manuscript is suited to Critical Care

The optimal timing of enteral nutrition after major abdominal surgery in the ICU remains an unsettled, everyday decision in critical care practice, yet the existing evidence base is dominated by trials conducted in medical and mixed ICUs that largely excluded surgical patients. We address this gap using the target-trial-emulation framework applied to the MIMIC-IV critical care database, with two prespecified co-primary comparisons that explicitly separate the *timing* estimand (early vs. late feeding among patients ultimately fed) from the *policy* estimand (early feeding vs. usual care in the full cohort). The key finding — a lower risk of antibiotic-treated infection with earlier feeding in the eventually-fed stratum, with a consistent direction but imprecise magnitude — is directly relevant to intensivists, surgeons, and nutrition support teams, and is reported transparently together with a full causal-inference sensitivity program (overlap weighting, IPTW, AIPW, propensity-score matching, negative-control outcomes, E-values, and same-center temporal and multicenter triangulations).

We believe the methodological approach — separating principal-stratum and policy estimands to avoid a spurious reversal of effect direction — will be of broad interest to the readership of *Critical Care*, and it provides a template for observational feeding-timing research beyond this dataset.
